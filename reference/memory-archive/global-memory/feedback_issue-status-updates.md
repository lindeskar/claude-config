A status comment on a tracking issue reports **where things stand**, not how you got there. The
reasoning lives in the working document; the comment carries conclusions and links.

**Why:** the audience is colleagues scanning for what changed. Evidence and argument make them hunt
for the finding, and an update nobody finishes is the same as no update.

**How to apply:**

- **Scannable structure, not prose.** A short framing line, then one heading per stream with two or
  three bullets. Use this even when the request says "a paragraph" — offer the structured version.
  (The opposite of [[question-sets-to-colleagues]], where numbered lists make an *ask* feel like a
  form. Structure suits status; plain lines suit questions.)
- **Report the finding, drop the evidence.** "Everything they're asking for should be public
  websites" beats a sentence listing the framework, the rulesets, the named owners and the review
  plan. Those justify the verdict in the catalogue entry; here they are noise.
- **Link the primary source rather than summarising it.** A PRD or thread the reader can open beats
  a paraphrase, and does not go stale.
- **Name the real disagreement plainly, in observational register.** *"It sounds more and more like
  the discussion is 'should we spend time on this' instead of 'how do we do this'"* surfaces the
  actual tension without accusing anyone, by describing the discussion rather than the people.
  **Do not pre-emptively soften this on the user's behalf** — they will make it explicit, and
  advising implicitness wastes the update's most useful sentence.
- **Cut to roughly half.** A first draft is consistently about twice the length that gets posted.
- Forward plans and dates are optional and often omitted deliberately; propose rather than assume.
