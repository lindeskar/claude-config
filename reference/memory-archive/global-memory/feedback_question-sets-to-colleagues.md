A chat message asking a colleague several questions is not a questionnaire and must not look like
one. The recipient should be able to reply in a sentence or two per point, or skip one.

**Why:** a form invites either a dutiful complete answer or no answer at all. Discovery needs the
easy, partial, honest reply — so every element that adds formality costs information.

**How to apply:**

- **No numbering.** One question per line, no blank lines between, no bullet markers. Numbered lists
  read as a form to be completed.
- **Ask for input, not answers.** "Ge gärna lite input på nedan när du har möjlighet" sets a far
  lower bar than "here are the questions, reply when you have a moment."
- **Offer a hypothesis to react to** rather than an open question — and rather than a list of your own
  guesses. "Känns som det kan växa mer än ren HTML?" and a parenthetical "(tänker att X löser en del
  av detta idag?)" both give the recipient something to correct, which is much cheaper to answer than
  an open prompt. Listing your own candidate answers instead reads as showing your homework.
- **No warm closer that carries a verdict.** A sign-off like "this looks easier than the other
  requests I've had" is an assessment, and it pre-empts a decision that has not been made. Warmth in
  the opener; nothing evaluative at the end.
- **Emoji suit opinion and pitch messages, not neutral information-gathering.** A question set to
  another department reads better with none. (Distinct from [[more-emoji-in-chat]], which is about
  direct chat replies.)
- **Quote the person's own words — never back-translate them.** When their phrase reaches you through
  someone else's English summary, ask for the original wording rather than rendering your own version
  back at them; a paraphrase they don't recognise makes it look like you weren't listening.
- **Keep the odd-question softener.** "Lite udda fråga; …" ahead of a question that will sound strange
  survives editing and is worth using.

**Swedish register:** the hedging particle *väl* does the work of "I'm guessing" more lightly than
*jag gissar att*; English loanwords are natural in a work context (*ha access till* over *kunna se*);
semicolons and commas read less fussy than em-dashes.
